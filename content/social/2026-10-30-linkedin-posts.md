## c1-70-30 — Правило 70/30 для поездки с друзьями (компания)

Зачем: Именованное правило PlanVIA, групповой угол, без цифр, которым нужен источник.

### EN

```
Six friends, one long weekend, and a group chat that has been "deciding" for three weeks.

Most group trips don't break on the destination. They break on the schedule: who does what, and for how long.

At PlanVIA, the AI group trip planner, we use a simple rule: 70/30.

→ 70% of the trip is shared. The dinner, the day trip, the one thing everyone came for.
→ 30% is free. Two go to the museum, three sleep in, one hunts for the record shop.

Why it works for groups:
• Nobody has to defend their whole wish list in the chat.
• Tired people get an exit that isn't "I'm skipping the whole day".
• Different budgets fit: the expensive tasting menu lives in the 30%, not in the shared plan.

How to start: plan the shared 70% first, day by day, so everyone can see it. Then leave real gaps in the plan, not "free time" at 11 pm.

Build the shared part in PlanVIA and share the plan with your group → goplanvia.com

What's your group's split?

#GroupTravel #TravelPlanning #TripWithFriends
```

### ES

```
Seis amigos, un puente y un chat de grupo que lleva tres semanas "decidiendo".

La mayoría de los viajes en grupo no se rompen por el destino. Se rompen por el horario: quién hace qué y durante cuánto tiempo.

En PlanVIA, el planificador de viajes en grupo con IA, usamos una regla sencilla: 70/30.

→ El 70% del viaje es compartido. La cena, la excursión, eso por lo que todos vinieron.

Por qué funciona en grupo:

• Quien está cansado tiene una salida que no es "paso de todo el día".
• Caben presupuestos distintos: el menú degustación caro va en el 30%, no en el plan común.

Cómo empezar: planifica primero el 70% común, día a día, para que todos lo vean. Después deja huecos reales en el plan, no "tiempo libre" a las 23:00.

```

```
French unions (CFDT, CGT, FO, UNSA and FSU) have called a nationwide day of strikes and protests on 5 November. Rail unions have also called a national SNCF strike for the same day. SNCF hasn't published which trains will run yet.

What it means for a group:
• Trains: TGV INOUI, Intercités, TER and Paris-region trains could run a reduced service (reported by media).
• Paris: a march is expected; the route wasn't set as of 21 October.
• The CGT has called to keep striking "on 5 November and after" (reported), so treat the 6th as uncertain too.
• The list of cancelled trains usually comes out the day before. That's little time to rebook six people.

What to do:
1. Don't put a fixed-time ticket or a tight connection on 5 November.
2. Choose flexible or refundable fares where you can.
3. Agree who checks SNCF Connect and the news on the evening of the 4th and updates the group.

We track strikes and entry rules for group trips at PlanVIA, the AI group trip planner. Sources in the first comment. Checked: 29 Oct 2026.

#TravelAlert #France #GroupTravel
```

## p2-ai-agents — Маркетинг командой из 9 AI-агентов: 3 правила (личный профиль)

### EN

```
I'm running PlanVIA's marketing with a team of 9 AI agents. Here are the 3 rules that make it work.

The setup: each agent has one job. One researches what people search for, one writes city pages, one fact-checks, one tracks strikes and entry rules, one looks for creators, one finds Reddit threads where a group needs help with a trip. They work in one shared repository, and I approve what goes out.

Rule 1: No source, no post.
Every price, opening time and entry rule needs a link to the original source and a check date. This week the fact-checker caught a draft that treated an old news banner as a fresh incident. It never went out.

Rule 2: Agents never post on Reddit.
They find threads and draft answers. I read the subreddit rules, post myself, as myself, and say that I work on PlanVIA.

Rule 3: Every message to a real person needs my yes.
Creators, journalists, partners: drafting is automatic, sending isn't.

What the agents are good at: research, first drafts, checking 30 facts without getting bored.
What they're not good at: knowing how our users actually feel. That part is still my job.

Built with Claude and n8n. I'll share what works and what doesn't as we go. Questions about the setup are welcome in the comments.
```

```
Think about how your last trip with friends got planned:
• a week of messages about dates
• a spreadsheet only one person updates
• three links to the same apartment
• someone asking "so how much per person?" the night before

The chat wins because everyone is already there. It's free, and nobody has to install anything.

Our answer so far: one plan everyone can open. Day by day, on a map, with real places, so the discussion starts from something concrete instead of a blank "where?".

The chat stays. The plan just stops living inside it.
```
