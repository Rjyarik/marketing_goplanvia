# EU Entry/Exit System: fingerprints and a face scan at the border for non-EU passports

**What changed:** Since 10 April 2026 the EU Entry/Exit System (EES) is fully operational and replaces passport stamping in 29 European countries. Every non-EU national on a short stay has their passport data, fingerprints and a facial image recorded at the external border, and each entry and exit is logged digitally.

**What it means for your group:**

- **Who queues:** only friends with non-EU passports (UK, US, Canada, Australia and so on). Citizens of EES countries, Cyprus and Ireland are not registered, and neither are non-EU friends who hold an EU residence permit or a long-stay visa.
- **First crossing takes longest:** the first time each non-EU traveller enters after the system went live, they are fully registered: passport scan, facial image and four fingerprints of the right hand. Later crossings are checks against the stored record.
- **Kids:** children under 12 give no fingerprints and, at Berlin BER, are registered at the staffed desk, not at the kiosks. Every child still gets their own EES record.
- **Queues:** Berlin BER warns of longer waiting times when entering or leaving the Schengen area, "even in the long term". Media reported multi-hour waits at peak dates this year (for example at Dover in late May 2026). That part is reported, not confirmed.
- **Mixed groups split up:** if 3 of 6 friends have EU passports, they will usually be through much faster while the other 3 queue. Your ground transfer has to wait for the slowest queue.
- **Where it doesn't apply:** Cyprus and Ireland do not use the EES, and passports are still stamped there.
- **How long data is kept:** 3 years after your last exit from the Schengen area, or 5 years if someone overstays.

**What to do:**

1. List who in the group has a non-EU passport and no EU residence permit. Only they go through EES.
2. Bring a biometric (chip) passport. BER's self-service kiosks only work with one.
3. Add a buffer after landing before any booked train, bus or rental car pickup, and arrive at the airport earlier than usual when you leave the Schengen area, especially at Christmas, Easter and on summer weekends.
4. Agree a meeting point after passport control so the EU-passport friends don't wait airside.
5. At BER, children under 12 are registered at the staffed desk, not at the kiosks, so a parent goes with them. Check your airport's EES page for its rules.
