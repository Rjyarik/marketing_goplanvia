# France nationwide strike and protest day on Thursday 5 November

**What changed:** On 5 October 2026 the CFDT published a joint call to "faire grève et manifester le jeudi 5 novembre", under the title "Appel à la mobilisation pour les salaires, les services publics, l'emploi et l'industrie". The first signatories are the CFDT, CGT, FO, UNSA and FSU. This is separate from the SNCF rail strike on the same day, which is covered in our SNCF entry.

**What it means for your group:**

- **More than trains:** the day is a strike across several sectors plus demonstrations. Media report that schools and public services (state, local authorities, hospitals) may be disrupted, depending on the notices each union files (reported, not confirmed).
- **Paris march:** a demonstration is expected in Paris, but as of 21 October the route was not set (reported). Streets near the march and nearby metro stops may be closed or crowded.
- **Transport beyond SNCF:** as of 21 October there was no RATP strike notice for 5 November, and the effect on RER and Transilien was undecided (reported).
- **It may not stop on the 5th:** the CGT has called to spread the strike "on 5 November and after" (reported, not confirmed). For a trip that starts or ends on 5 November, treat 6 November as uncertain too.
- **Sites and services:** we found no official list of affected museums, monuments or public offices. If your group has a booked slot on 5 November, check that venue's own website.

**What to do:**

1. If you are in France on 5 November, check your train (SNCF Connect), RATP and Île-de-France Mobilités the day before, and keep a plan B for getting around the city.
2. Don't schedule a fixed-time ticket or a connection on 5 November that you can't move. Where your fare allows it, choose a flexible or refundable option.
3. In Paris, check the Paris Police Prefecture for the march route once it is published, and plan your day around it.
4. Agree in the group chat who watches the news on the evening of 4 November, so one person updates everyone.
